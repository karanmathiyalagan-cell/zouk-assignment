Make Your Own Set: UX and AOV audit

The builder’s job is to sell a matching set: one bag, then a wallet, pouch and charm in the same print. The structure supports that. A box you fill slot by slot, a running total and a nudge before paying are the right pieces. But the screens don’t deliver the matching yet. Every step shows office bags, none leads with the bag’s print, the tray total is wrong, and the no-discount screens the test will ship aren’t drawn yet. Fix those and the A/B test will measure what the feature is meant to do.

Product decisions · 26 Oct 2026

What this audit assumes

  • No discounts in the first version. The builder ships without any box discount and is A/B tested first. If it doesn’t convert well enough, the next lever is gifts (for example gift wrap or a charm), not a price cut.
  • The discount screens stay in the file. They are kept on purpose as a ready-made variant in case discounts are switched on later. This audit treats them as Variant B, not as something to remove.
  • The reason to use it is a matching look. Shoppers come to put the same print across different products: bag, wallet, pouch, charm.
  • Savings are shown as real MRP savings, added up. Each item shows its MRP struck through and its sale price (e.g. ₹2,000 → ₹1,500, you save ₹500). The tray and review add these up across the box, so a full set shows the full saving. The strike price is always the item’s real MRP, and the saving is labelled as savings on MRP, never as a combo saving (see A3).

What already works

The slot tray shows the goal

Empty “+” slots next to filled thumbnails make the set feel unfinished. That pull toward completion is the main order-value engine when there’s no discount, so keep it.

“Everything else will be matched to it”

Step 1’s subtitle already states the value: pick the hero bag, the rest follows. The steps after it just need to keep that promise.

A rail of extras on review

The Box of 2 and 3 reviews end with one-tap picks. Change the reason from “save more” to “complete your set” and it stays the strongest upsell in the file.

An exit that doesn’t trap people

“Check out now” plus a confirm step lets someone leave with 2 or 3 items instead of abandoning. Fewer lost orders, as long as the dialog sells the rest of the set (see A5).

Built from the PLP

The product card, filter bar and badges come from the Office Bags PLP, so the builder feels like the same site.

Box of 3 marked Most popular

A sensible default that also gives the test a clear starting point.

A · Pricing, savings and the offer (product)

BlockerA1

The tray total is wrong at almost every step

Box of 4 / Steps 2–4 (both states) · Box of 3 / Steps 2–3 · Box of 2 / Step 2 · all three review screens

The total doesn’t change when a product is added: “Step 2 · Product added” still reads ₹1,499 with two bags in the box. The struck-through price jumps between unrelated numbers (₹1,874, ₹2,998, ₹3,371), and the third bag adds only ₹299. None of the tray totals match the review screen.

Why it matters. The running total is the reason the tray exists. A shopper who sees ₹2,697 and then ₹4,796.80 one tap later reads it as a bait-and-switch, at the most expensive point in the funnel to lose her.

Fix

With no discount the rule is simple, so state it in the spec: total = sum of sale prices, struck price = sum of MRPs, you save = the difference. Update on every add or remove. Add a “Product added” frame for every step with real numbers so developers have a reference.

HighA2

The discount variant is drawn; the no-discount version isn’t

Menus · Box of 4 · With milestones · review screens · exit dialogs

Keeping the discount screens as a ready variant is a good call. The gap is that they are the only version drawn. These six places exist only with a discount:

  • “Save more” tag on the Women menu and on the Sale menu.
  • The milestones bar (free shipping at 2, gift wrap at 3, “20% off” at 4).
  • “20% / 15% / 10% OFF” on every review row, with prices like ₹1,199.20.
  • “Add more & save more · Add 1 more product to get 15% off” on the Box of 2 and 3 reviews.
  • “You are saving ₹1,199.20 on this combo.” in the pay bar.
  • The tray’s “You saved” figures, which are computed as a combo discount.

Developers building the test will have no spec for the tray, review, exit dialog or menu without a discount. If they copy these frames, shoppers get promised a discount they won’t receive, and the test stops measuring a no-discount builder.

Fix

Group the discount frames in a section labelled “Variant B · Discounts (not in test)” and draw the no-discount versions of those six places for the test build, using the copy in D2.

Before Variant B is ever switched on
  • Pick one model. The file has two: the milestones bar (gift wrap at 3, 20% at 4) and 10 / 15 / 20% by box size. The tray also applies 20% from the second item.
  • Make it beat the sitewide “Flat 15% off above ₹2,500”, or let it stack. As drawn, Box of 2 at 10% costs ₹2,698.20, which is ₹149.90 more than the same two bags with the banner. Box of 3 at 15% costs the same.
  • Drop “Free shipping” from the milestones. Zouk already ships every prepaid order free.
  • Use whole rupees, not ₹1,199.20.

The milestones bar also works as the design for the gift test if that comes first.

HighA3

Show added-up savings on MRP, and label them honestly

Tray · review pay bar · control arm’s cart

Adding up each item’s MRP-to-sale saving across the box (₹1,500 per bag here, ₹6,000 for a full box) is standard practice, and Zouk’s PLP already does it (“You’re saving ₹799 · 3 items added”). A bigger number at checkout does make the order feel like a good deal. Two rules keep it on the right side of the line:

  • The struck price is the item’s real MRP, the same one shown on its product page. Never set a higher strike price for the builder.
  • Say where the saving comes from. “You save ₹6,000 on MRP”, not “on this combo” or “combo savings”. The same prices are available outside the builder, so crediting the box with them is a misleading price claim. The CCPA’s 2023 dark-pattern guidelines are aimed at exactly this, and shoppers who compare tabs will notice.

For the A/B test. If the builder shows a large “you save” figure and the control cart doesn’t, any lift may come from that line rather than from the builder. Show the same added-up savings line in the control arm’s cart so the test isolates the builder.

Fix

Total ₹5,996 ₹11,996
You save ₹6,000 on MRP

Use the PLP’s green savings style (C1) and whole rupees.

HighA4

The entry point sells savings, not the look

Menu / Women · Menu / Sale

The menu row says “Save more” and lists Box of 2, 3 and 4 with nothing about what goes in them. Without a discount, the reason to tap is the matching set, and the menu shows none of it. It also sits under Sale, next to BOGO deals, which sets a deal expectation the builder won’t meet.

Fix

Describe the set, not the saving:

Match your print · NEW
Box of 2 · Bag + 1 matching piece
Box of 3 · Bag + wallet + 1 more · Most popular
Box of 4 · Bag + wallet + pouch + charm

Add a homepage or PLP banner with a real same-print set photo (the Flow page planned both). Keep the Sale entry for the test, but track the two entry points separately: Sale traffic may expect a deal.

HighA5

The exit dialog doesn’t show what the set is missing

Overlay / Confirm · 2 products, 3 products, 2 of 3 products

“Checkout with 2 of 4 products?” is a yes/no question with no pictures. “Add more products” drops the shopper back into a grid. Without a discount, the pull to stay is the unfinished look, and the dialog doesn’t show it.

Fix

Show the gap, in her print, with one-tap adds inside the sheet:

Your FloMotif set is missing a pouch and a charm
[ pouch thumbnail · ₹699 · + Add ] [ charm thumbnail · ₹399 · + Add ]
[ Complete my set ] [ Pay ₹2,998 for 2 ]

Prices in the copy are illustrative.

HighA6

Turn “Add more & save more” into “Complete your set”

Box of 2 / Preview · Box of 3 / Preview

The rail is the right idea, but it recommends more office bags in other prints and gives a discount as the reason. It’s also unclear that tapping “+” turns a Box of 2 into a Box of 3.

Fix

Fill the rail with the missing categories in the bag’s print, titled “Complete your FloMotif set”. After an add, confirm with a toast: “Added. Your box is now a Box of 3.” Show the same rail on the Box of 4 review if any category in that print hasn’t been used.

MediumA7

Box of 4 review has nothing after the list

Box of 4 / Preview · Review your combo

Once the box is full there is no further offer. The Flow page already planned personalised initials (+₹149), which is a paid extra and fits the no-discount rule.

Fix

Add one or two paid extras under the list: initials on the main bag (+₹149) and “Start another box” for gifting. Keep free gift wrap back for the Phase 2 gift test so the first version stays clean.

MediumA8

“Proceed to pay” skips the cart, and the cart already has 3 items

All review screens · header cart badge

The header shows 3 items in the cart. It’s unclear whether “Proceed to pay” checks out the box alone or the whole cart. The Flow page planned for box items to land in the cart as a group with “Edit combo”; the Final page jumps straight to payment.

Fix

Add the box to the cart as a group (cart offers keep working and people can keep shopping), with “Pay now” as the express option. Tag each box item with a box ID as a cart attribute so the test can attribute orders.

B · Flow and interaction (UX)

BlockerB1

Every step shows office bags

Steps 2–4 in Box of 2, 3 and 4 · review screens

“Add a wallet”, “Add a pouch or organiser” and “Finish with a charm” all show ₹1,399–₹1,499 women’s office bags tagged “Fits 15″ Laptop” and “Top pick for office”. Step 1’s filter chips (“Bestseller”, “Up to 15″”) carry over to every step. The review ends with four office bags in four prints, the opposite of a matching set.

Why it matters. A matching look across categories is now the whole reason the feature exists. It is also what lifts order value without a discount: one bag plus cheaper, easy extras in the same print is a much easier yes than four bags.

Fix

Give each step its own grid and filters: wallets (zip, bi-fold, card case), pouches and organisers, charms. Reset filters between steps. Even if the Final page is a mock, these frames will be read as the spec.

BlockerB2

“Same print first” isn’t delivered

Step 2 subtitle · Steps 2–4 grids

After picking the Flomotif bag, Step 2’s first results are Mughal Art and Bidri Kaiser, and Steps 3–4 don’t lead with Flomotif either. Step 4 offers the Flomotif bag again, already in the box, with no “In your box” state, so duplicates are possible.

Fix

Pre-select a “Same print as your bag” chip and pin those items first with a small “Same print” tag. Mark anything already in the box. Show the set taking shape: the tray thumbnails all in one print say more than any copy. Decide the fallback for prints that don’t exist in every category (see open questions).

HighB3

No size switch, no save, and the whole site is one tap away

Header on all builder frames · Scroll Area layers “Press Strip” and “Site Footer”

The Flow page specified a size chip, a “Leave your combo?” sheet that saves the box for 7 days, and a quick view. The Final page dropped all three. Meanwhile the builder keeps the full site header (menu, search, wishlist, cart) and the press strip and site footer under the grid. Each one is an exit with no way back to a half-built box.

Fix

Use a focused header: back arrow, “Box of 4 ▾” switcher, close with a save-draft sheet. Drop the footer inside the builder. Bring back “Continue your box (2)” in the menu for saved drafts.

HighB4

You can’t remove or swap from the tray

Box Tray component · product card “✓ ADDED” state

Filled slots are plain thumbnails with no tap action or remove control. The card switches to “✓ ADDED” with no undo. Editing only exists on the review screen. Empty slots are unlabelled, so the tray doesn’t say what comes next.

Fix

Tap a filled slot to get Swap / Remove / View. Let “Added ✓” toggle back. Label the slots (Bag, Wallet, Pouch, Charm) so the tray doubles as a progress map.

MediumB5

Previous and Next don’t say much

Box Tray, all steps

On Step 1, a disabled “Previous” takes half the bar. A disabled “Next” doesn’t say why. The Flow page had better labels: “Next: Wallet →” and “Pick a wallet to continue”.

Fix

Move back to the header arrow. Give the single primary button a specific label: “Pick a wallet to continue” while disabled, “Next: Pouch” when enabled, “Review your set” on the last step.

MediumB6

Fixed bars take a third of the screen

Header (124px) + Box Tray (136px) on an 844px frame

260px of fixed bars, plus the step header and two filter rows, leave room for about one row of products on Step 1.

Fix

Hide the promo bar inside the builder and shrink the tray to a ~64px bar (slots + total + button) that expands on tap.

MediumB7

Frames disagree with each other

Step 1 vs Step 1 · Product added · Box of 4 · With milestones · review “+” circles

  • Step 1’s second card is Mughal Art in the empty state and FloLov once a product is added.
  • The milestones frame ticks three milestones with three items, shows a check over the second tray thumbnail, and disables Next.
  • The “+” circles between review rows look like buttons but are decoration (no hotspot). Remove them or make them insert an item.
Fix

One QA pass so each “added” frame is its empty frame plus exactly one change.

C · UI consistency (vs. the rest of the file and zouk.co.in)

HighC1

Savings are shown in red here and green on the PLP

Review pay bar · tray “You saved” pill · vs. 06 PLP / Women’s Office Bags (Web/Mobile Navigation page)

The builder writes its saving in red and puts “You saved ₹375” in a maroon pill. The PLP in the same file uses green with a check: “You’re saving ₹799”. Red reads as an error or a price increase. “Saved” is also the wrong tense before payment. Since the added-up saving is now the main money message (A3), it needs the clearest treatment.

Fix

Use the PLP’s green savings treatment everywhere and write “You save ₹6,000 on MRP”.

MediumC2

Four button styles in one flow

Product card · Box Tray · review pay bar · PLP sticky bar

Cards use uppercase “ADD TO BAG” with a small radius. The tray uses sentence-case full pills (“Previous”, “Next”). The review uses a pill “Proceed to pay”. The PLP’s sticky bar uses uppercase “VIEW CART →”. The live site’s primary button has a 4px radius.

Fix

One primary and one secondary button spec for the builder, matched to the site’s current button.

MediumC3

Three different reference prices for the same bag

Product cards · tray · review screens

The struck price is MRP (₹2,999) on cards, ₹1,874 in the tray and the sale price (₹1,499) on review. The review also shows paise (₹1,199.20), which only exist because of the old discount maths. The live site and the rest of this file use whole rupees.

Fix

MRP is the only struck price, everywhere, matching the product page (the site standard: “Regular price ₹2,499 · 51% OFF”). Whole rupees throughout; with the discount gone this happens on its own.

MediumC4

Badges, chips and titles drift

  • “Limited stoc” is clipped on several cards.
  • “Price drop” is sentence case here and “PRICE DROP” on the PLP frame.
  • The “STEP 1 OF 4” chip has wider letter-spacing in the “Product added” frames than in the empty ones, which suggests a detached instance.
  • The review title is centred; every other screen title is left-aligned.
  • Product names truncate on the Box of 2 and 3 reviews but wrap on Box of 4.
Fix

Re-link the badge and step-chip instances to their components, use auto-width badges, and left-align the review title.

MediumC5

The exit dialog breaks the mobile pattern

Overlay / Confirm frames (720px tall vs. 844px elsewhere)

A centred modal on an opaque near-black backdrop, with no close control. The Flow page used bottom sheets for the same kind of decision, which suits one-handed use and leaves room for the add-on thumbnails in A5.

Fix

Bottom sheet over a translucent scrim with a close button, on an 844px frame like the rest.

MediumC6

Six names for one feature

Page: “Make Your Own Set”. Menu: “Build your own combo”. Flow page: “Make your own combo”. Screens: “Box of 4”. Review: “Review your combo”. And the Sale menu already has “Combos & Sets” for fixed, discounted combos, which invites confusion.

Fix

Pick one name that says what the shopper gets, and use it from the menu to the order confirmation. With the matching look as the pitch, “Match your print” or “Build your set” fits better than “combo”, and stays clear of “Combos & Sets”.

MediumC7

Stale details from the live site

The app banner reads “4.5 ★ | 5L+ Downloads”; the live site says “4.6 ⭐ | 10L+ Downloads”. The FloMotif Women’s Office Bag is ₹1,214 (MRP ₹2,499) live versus ₹1,499 (₹2,999) in the file. Fine for a mock, but update before these screens go into a spec or a pitch.

A/B test design

Builder vs. no builder

Split: randomise by user. Control sees the site without the builder entry points; treatment sees them. Both arms keep the sitewide banner and the same added-up savings line in the cart (A3).

Primary: revenue per visitor and AOV across the whole arm, plus items per order. Compare arms, not builder users vs. everyone else: people who open the builder were already more likely to buy more.

Secondary: builder funnel (entry → first pick → each step → review → paid), set completion rate, share of box items in the bag’s print, exit-sheet “complete my set” rate.

Guardrails: overall conversion rate, returns and exchanges on box items, shift away from BOGO and Special Combos.

Before launch

Write down when gifts come in

Agree on these before the test starts, so the gift decision isn’t made after looking at the numbers:

  • The smallest AOV lift worth shipping, and the sample size and run time that can detect it (full weeks only).
  • Which result triggers the gift variant: for example, builder conversion below a set level, or no significant AOV lift by the end date.
  • Keep the run clear of big sales like the Big Bag Festival, which would swamp the effect.
  • Next test if triggered: gift by box size (the parked milestones design) vs. no gift.

For the business

Open questions to settle before design continues

  • Does the sitewide “15% off above ₹2,500” apply to builder orders? If it does, show it in the tray and review so totals match checkout.
  • Can BOGO items go into a box, and at which price? BOGO pricing only works in pairs.
  • When a print doesn’t exist in a category (no charm in that print), what’s the fallback: a complementary print, or a neutral solid?
  • Can a box hold two bags, or is it strictly one bag plus accessories?
  • Does “Proceed to pay” include items already in the cart?
  • Is the MRP shown in the builder pulled from the same source as the product page, so the two can never differ?
